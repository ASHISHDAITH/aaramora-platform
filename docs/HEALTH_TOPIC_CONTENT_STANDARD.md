# Health and Wellbeing Topic Content Standard

Every health, wellbeing, relationship and women's-health topic should answer the same questions in plain language:

1. **What is this?** One short definition; say whether the term is a diagnosis, symptom, normal response, research concept or informal expression.
2. **How might it feel?** Common emotional, cognitive, physical and day-to-day experiences. Say that people vary; avoid presenting a checklist as a diagnosis.
3. **What may help?** Distinguish self-care that can support wellbeing from evidence-based treatments delivered by qualified professionals. Never prescribe medication, doses or supplement regimens.
4. **When should someone seek support?** Give clear non-urgent, prompt and urgent routes. Never infer that all symptoms are caused by a mental-health condition; point out when medical causes need assessment.
5. **What is uncertain?** Explain evidence limitations, study design and what has not been proven.
6. **Sources and review** Named author, qualified reviewer, citations, last-reviewed date, expiry/next review date, license information and correction route.

## Voice
- Speak to the visitor without blaming, diagnosing or patronising.
- Use “may”, “can” and “some people” where experiences differ.
- Use “what may help” rather than “how to cure” or “guaranteed healing”.
- Avoid reducing a person to a condition.
- Respect cultural and language differences; Hindi, Marathi and Romanised Hindi require native-speaker and clinician review.
- No forced disclosure, trauma retelling or unnecessary personal data.

## High-risk boundaries
- Mental health self-reflection is not diagnosis or treatment.
- “Stress attack” is not a formal diagnosis; describe an acute stress surge and differentiate panic-like episodes and medical symptoms.
- Burnout is an occupational phenomenon in ICD-11, not a medical condition and not a label for every form of exhaustion.
- Trauma-related symptoms do not automatically mean PTSD.
- Intrusive thoughts do not automatically mean OCD.
- Attachment protest is a relationship concept, not a diagnostic label.
- Relationship communication advice is inappropriate as a default where coercion, violence or fear is present.
- PMOS (formerly PCOS) is the updated name; retain PCOS and PCOD as search aliases during the transition. Do not state that PCOD is a separate milder standard diagnosis.
- Brain-health associations in PMOS/PCOS are emerging and mixed. Do not claim causation, brain damage, dementia or a personal prognosis.
- Postpartum psychosis is a medical emergency; no self-help or routine appointment pathway should delay urgent assessment.

## Per-topic content pattern
Use these visible headings consistently:
- “How it may feel”
- “What may help”
- “When to seek support”
- “What is still uncertain” (when the evidence is emerging or mixed)

Add a short “what this page cannot tell you” note for every quiz or symptom guide.


## Relationship-concept pages
Use how it may feel, what may help, when to seek support and a status note (research dimension, clinical construct, emerging concept or popular label). Do not turn descriptive concepts into diagnostic quizzes. Preserve source cautions and the difference between ordinary attachment needs and coercive or harmful behaviour.
