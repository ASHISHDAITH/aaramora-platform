# First-Visit Journeys and Information Architecture

## Journey A — Visitor arrives overwhelmed

1. Sees the support route and service boundary immediately.
2. Reads one calm, non-demanding headline.
3. Chooses a small next step, looks around, or leaves.
4. If choosing a pause, sees alternatives and can skip.
5. Reaches a guide or human service without registration.

**Do not:** ask for trauma history, require a mood score, start a chatbot, require consent to tracking, or force an onboarding sequence.

## Journey B — Visitor wants to understand a feeling

1. Selects a plain-language topic.
2. Gets a direct short answer near the top.
3. Sees what is known, what is uncertain and what could be a useful next step.
4. Sees when professional help may be worth considering.
5. Sees author, qualified reviewer, references, review date and correction route.

## Journey C — Visitor wants human help

1. Finds Tele-MANAS and emergency route at the top and again on the support page.
2. Understands the service is external and the platform is not an emergency service.
3. Gets clear explanations of psychologist / psychiatrist / counsellor roles.
4. Can prepare questions for an appointment.
5. Can browse the Access Map without creating an account.

## Journey D — Visitor supports someone else

1. Selects “I'm supporting someone”.
2. Gets short scripts and practical listening suggestions.
3. Learns how to avoid pressure or promises.
4. Gets a route to professional and emergency support.
5. No anonymous venting feed, public disclosure or forced sharing.

## Main navigation

- Explore — reviewed guides.
- A small pause — optional, non-clinical prompts.
- Find support — appointment preparation and India Access Map.
- Women's health — PMOS, endometriosis, menstrual concerns, menopause, thyroid, iron deficiency and perinatal mental health.
- Our promises — reviewers, privacy, corrections and governance.

## First-release pages

1. First-visit homepage.
2. Find support in India.
3. Prepare for a first appointment.
4. Overthinking / anxiety / stress information.
5. Sleep information and a local-only sleep diary.
6. Words Library and supporter guides.
7. Trust Ledger and privacy.

## Story design

The Kiran story is a short fictional metaphor about choice and pace, not a trauma narrative or testimonial. Avoid “before/after” recovery arcs. No personal tragedy is used as an emotional hook. The story can be skipped; it must never be an onboarding prerequisite.


## Journey E — Visitor has a women's health question

1. Can reach Women's health directly from primary navigation.
2. Chooses a topic without answering a diagnostic quiz.
3. Reads plain-language signs, what may help, uncertainty and when to seek assessment.
4. Sees a clear route to prepare for an appointment.
5. Can read sources and reviewer information.
6. Can leave without saving, signing up or sharing private health history.

PMOS page shows **PMOS (formerly PCOS)** and keeps PCOS as a search synonym while the terminology transitions.

## Journey F — Visitor has a relationship question

1. Open the separate Relationships corner without sign-up.
2. Read the boundary note: education and reflection, not diagnosis or therapy.
3. See the safety-first route before self-help communication advice.
4. Choose a guide or optional reflection quiz.
5. View only selected behaviour themes, with no score, label or partner assessment.
6. Use direct, respectful communication scripts only if safe.
7. Reach human support where fear, coercion, threats, sexual pressure or violence is present.

Quiz answers are not stored, transmitted, analysed or used for advertising. Community remains outside the current build.
