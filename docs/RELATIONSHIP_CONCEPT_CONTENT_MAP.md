## Interpretive cautions from the report
- Adult attachment is commonly measured along anxiety and avoidance dimensions. They are not diagnoses or fixed identities; they may vary by relationship, context and time.
- Attachment protest is a descriptive umbrella, not a formal psychiatric diagnosis. More precise terms include attachment-system activation, hyperactivating strategies, proximity seeking, excessive reassurance seeking and separation distress.
- A normal need for comfort, contact, reassurance or repair is not automatically a symptom or manipulation.
- The same behaviour can have different motives. Repeated monitoring, threats and intimidation must be judged by impact, consent and safety, not excused as attachment protest.
- A delayed reply alone cannot prove rejection, cheating, avoidant attachment, loss of love or manipulation.
- Pursuer–distancer and demand–withdraw are dyadic cycles, not fixed identities.
- Attachment injury, betrayal, ambiguous loss and breakup grief are distinct from ordinary protest.
- Trauma bonding is reserved for harmful dynamics involving abuse, control, fear, manipulation or severe neglect; not ordinary longing or hot-and-cold messaging.
- Limerence is an emerging research/descriptive term, not a formal diagnosis.
- Relationship OCD symptoms require separate assessment of obsessions/compulsions, time burden, distress and impairment.
- Coercive control restricts autonomy through intimidation, pressure, surveillance, isolation, threats or other controlling behaviour.
- Forgiveness does not require reconciliation. Safety, accountability and consent take priority.

## Practical pathways from pages 10–11 of the report
### Person experiencing protest urges
State the observable trigger without interpretation; identify the feared story and treat it as a possibility, not a fact; regulate before responding; make one direct, proportionate request; avoid repeated calls, surveillance, threats and checking rituals; maintain routines, friendships and outside support.

### Partner receiving protest behaviour
Acknowledge emotion without accepting an inaccurate accusation; give clear information where possible; if a pause is needed, say when the conversation can resume; follow through; maintain boundaries around insults, threats, surveillance and unwanted contact. Do not promise constant availability or take responsibility for regulating every emotion.

### Both people
Agree on communication expectations, what a pause means, how to restart a difficult conversation and which behaviours are unacceptable. Focus on the shared cycle rather than labels. A repair includes what happened, impact, responsibility, required change and a time to review whether the change occurred.

**Safety boundary:** These steps are for relationships where both people can participate safely and voluntarily. Where there is intimidation, stalking, coercive control, violence, threats or fear of retaliation, prioritise individual safety and specialist support. Do not use attachment language to pressure someone to stay, respond, forgive or resume contact.
