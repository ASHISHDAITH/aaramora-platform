# Mental Health and Women's Health Topic Research — 24 November 2026

This evidence register supports the new mental-wellbeing guide and updates to the women's-health corner. It is a curated first pass, not a formal systematic review. All pages require named clinical review before publication.

## Mental health: experience and support

### Depression
WHO's September 2026 fact sheet describes depression as more than everyday mood variation, involving depressed mood or loss of interest/pleasure over time and effects across work, study and relationships. It lists effective psychological treatments, including behavioural activation, CBT, interpersonal psychotherapy and problem-solving therapy; medicines may be considered by clinicians depending on severity and individual factors. The website describes experiences and routes to care, not personal diagnosis or medication selection.
- WHO: https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/Depression
- NICE adult depression guideline (reviewed January 2026): https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng222

### Anxiety
WHO's September 2026 fact sheet describes persistent, excessive fear/worry, physical tension, cognitive and behavioural symptoms, and interference with daily life. Psychological interventions, particularly CBT-based approaches, have strong evidence for several anxiety disorders. The guide names symptoms but stresses that new physical symptoms can have other causes.
- WHO: https://www.who.int/en/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/anxiety-disorders

### Panic attacks and panic disorder
NHS information describes sudden intense mental and physical symptoms such as racing heart, dizziness, sweating, nausea, breathlessness, trembling and fear. The NHS also states that similar symptoms can arise from other conditions, so a first or unusual episode should not automatically be self-labelled as panic. Recurrent attacks and persistent worry warrant clinical assessment; CBT-based treatment is used for panic disorder.
- NHS anxiety/fear/panic: https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/feelings-symptoms-behaviours/feelings-and-symptoms/anxiety-fear-panic/
- NHS panic disorder: https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/conditions/panic-disorder/

### Acute stress surges
“Stress attack” is informal wording, not a formal diagnostic term. WHO's 2020 guide, *Doing What Matters in Times of Stress*, teaches grounding, unhooking from unkind thoughts, making room for feelings, values-based action and kindness. WHO's 2026 implementation manual describes structured psychological self-help interventions including this guide and Step-by-Step.
- WHO stress guide: https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240003927
- WHO 2026 delivery manual: https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240120785
- WHO stress Q&A: https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/stress

### Trauma-related distress / PTSD
NHS materials list intrusive memories, nightmares, avoidance, mood changes and other symptoms; trauma-focused CBT and EMDR are examples of treatments offered by trained professionals for PTSD. Aaramora must not equate exposure to trauma with a PTSD diagnosis or pressure a visitor to retell an event.
- NHS PTSD: https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/conditions/ptsd-post-traumatic-stress-disorder/
- SAMHSA trauma-informed principles: https://www.samhsa.gov/mental-health/trauma-violence/trauma-informed-approaches-programs

### Sleep problems
NHS guidance recommends assessment when insomnia persists, affects daily functioning or does not improve with changes; CBT may be offered. Sleep medication advice must be left to a clinician/pharmacist.
- NHS insomnia: https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/insomnia/

### Burnout
WHO ICD-11 classifies burnout as an occupational phenomenon arising from chronic workplace stress, with exhaustion, mental distance/cynicism about work and reduced professional efficacy. It is not a medical condition and should not be applied to other life areas as a blanket label.
- WHO: https://www.who.int/standards/classifications/frequently-asked-questions/burn-out-an-occupational-phenomenon

### OCD and intrusive thoughts
The site must distinguish unwanted intrusive thoughts from OCD. OCD requires a broader pattern of obsessions and/or compulsions that causes distress or impairment; a short website quiz cannot determine it. Refer to a trained clinician. ERP-based CBT is an established treatment approach; do not use reassurance loops as the website's core coping tool.

## Women's health: PMOS, PCOS and PCOD terminology

On 12 May 2026, the international name changed from polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) to **polyendocrine metabolic ovarian syndrome (PMOS)**. The 2026 updated international guideline describes the change as nomenclature; underlying clinical features, diagnostic criteria and management recommendations did not change in that update. ESHRE supports a transition period during which old and new terms coexist. Use “PMOS (formerly PCOS)” in visible headings and retain PCOS/PCOD search aliases so users can find older reports and familiar local wording.
- Monash PMOS guideline: https://www.monash.edu/medicine/mchri/pcos/guideline
- Updated 2026 international guideline PDF: https://www.monash.edu/__data/assets/pdf_file/0009/4360095/Updated-PMOS-Guideline-12-June26.pdf
- Endocrine Society announcement: https://www.endocrine.org/news-and-advocacy/news-room/2026/pcos-name-change
- ESHRE position: https://www.eshre.eu/Guidelines-and-Legal/Position-statements/PCOS-Name-Change
- Lancet consensus nomenclature paper: https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(26)00717-8

“PCOD” is used colloquially in India but is not established as a separate, internationally standardised milder diagnosis distinct from PMOS/PCOS. The website should not tell visitors that PCOD is a different disease. Explain that a report using PCOD/PCOS needs interpretation by a clinician using symptoms and accepted criteria.
- India MoHFW standard treatment guideline (older PCOS terminology): https://clinicalestablishments.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/standard-treatment-guidelines/3601.pdf
- International guideline recommendations: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10477934/

## PMOS/PCOS, mental health and memory: mixed evidence

The 2023 international evidence-based guideline recommends clinicians remain alert to depression and anxiety among adolescents and adults with PCOS, using regionally validated screening tools, and further assess/refer/treat where moderate or severe symptoms are found.
- 2023 guideline recommendations: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10477934/

The 2024 CARDIA cohort study reported associations between PCOS and lower performance on some midlife cognitive measures and lower white-matter fractional anisotropy in a smaller MRI subgroup. The authors noted limitations, including the small number of participants meeting PCOS criteria and the inability to establish cause.
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38295344/
- Open full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11383880/

A 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis included 22 studies, with 136,008 participants with PMOS and 333,895 without. Its pooled associations in executive function, attention and working memory were small and statistically non-significant, with heterogeneity in some domains. This makes the overall cognition evidence mixed and still developing.
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42396950/
- Open full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13329973/

Required wording: “Some studies have reported associations with midlife cognitive measures, but the overall evidence is mixed and does not show that PMOS causes memory loss or brain damage. If cognitive symptoms are troublesome, seek assessment rather than assuming PMOS is the cause.” Never give an individual dementia prognosis or promise that diet/supplements prevent cognitive decline.

## Endometriosis
WHO's 15 October 2025 fact sheet says endometriosis may involve severe menstrual pain, heavy bleeding, chronic pelvic pain, infertility, abdominal symptoms and impacts on sexual, bowel, urinary and mental health. There is no definitive cure; treatment is individualised to symptoms, preferences, side effects, cost and pregnancy plans.
- WHO: https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/endometriosis

## PMS and PMDD
NHS guidance explains that cyclical symptoms should be tracked over time; PMDD diagnosis considers repeated monthly symptoms and their impact. Seek a professional assessment rather than treating severe symptoms as “just hormones”.
- NHS PMDD: https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/pmdd-premenstrual-dysphoric-disorder/

## Postpartum depression, anxiety and psychosis
Postpartum depression/anxiety warrant assessment and support. Postpartum psychosis is a medical emergency and may include sudden confusion, hallucinations, delusions, mania or rapidly changing mood, often in the first two weeks after birth. Same-day emergency assessment is required; do not direct users to a self-help-only pathway.
- NHS postpartum psychosis: https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/conditions/post-partum-psychosis/

## Editorial and safety rules
- Use “what may help”, not “cure” or guaranteed “healing”.
- No medicine doses, supplement stacks or individual treatment instructions.
- Distinguish an ordinary stress surge from panic disorder, PTSD, depression or medical emergencies.
- No condition should be diagnosed from symptoms on the site.
- Every page has an author, reviewer, reviewed date, expiry/next review date and references.
- Hindi/Marathi/Hinglish drafts require human language and clinical review.
