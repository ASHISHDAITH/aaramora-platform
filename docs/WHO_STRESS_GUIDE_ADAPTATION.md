## What is verified from the WHO publication page

The official page describes the guide as an evidence-informed and field-tested self-help guide for coping with stress and links accompanying audio exercises. Its page lists a Hindi edition. The currently displayed language/audio list did not show Marathi; that is a catalogue observation on 24 November 2026, not proof that no Marathi file exists elsewhere.

WHO Self-Help Plus (SH+) is a different, five-session group course for up to 30 adults, delivered by supervised non-specialist facilitators using pre-recorded audio and an illustrated guide. It is not a self-guided website page. Do not present AARAMORA as SH+ or claim equivalent outcomes.

### A small pause when stress is present

This is an optional reflection, not a way to remove stress on demand. You can skip any step, keep your eyes open, change position or stop.

**1. Notice the place you are in**

Look around and name one ordinary object, one colour or one sound. If looking around is not comfortable, choose another way to orient yourself or skip this step.

**2. Give the experience a simple name**

Without explaining the whole story, you might quietly name what is present: “worry”, “pressure”, “sadness”, “anger”, “uncertainty”, or “I do not know”. A name is a description, not a diagnosis.

**3. Make a little room**

You do not have to like the feeling or agree with the thoughts that accompany it. Notice whether you can allow the experience to be present for a moment without forcing it away. If this makes things worse, stop and choose an external activity or contact someone.

**4. Remember what matters in this moment**

Choose one quality you would like to bring to the next small action: care, honesty, patience, dignity, connection, learning or safety. This is a personal choice, not a moral test.

**5. Choose a small, safe next step**

The next step may be asking for help, drinking water, taking a break, making a practical plan, or doing nothing until you feel ready. You do not have to complete all five steps.

**6. Speak to yourself as you would to someone you care about**

Try a factual, non-judging sentence such as: “This is difficult right now; I can take one step or ask for help.” Do not use forced positivity or promise a particular outcome.

### Boundaries

- This page is not psychotherapy, ACT, psychological first aid, treatment, risk assessment or a substitute for clinical or emergency care.
- Do not use a grounding exercise to delay help for urgent danger, suicidal intent, medical emergencies or violence.
- The page must not imply that stress is caused by a failure to think positively or practise correctly.
- No timer, streak, forced breathing, trauma narration, score or personalised treatment advice.
- Use a transcript for any audio. Audio must not autoplay and must include visible pause and stop controls.

## Hindi and Marathi availability check

- WHO's publication page lists a Hindi edition of the written guide.
- The page's listed audio exercise languages did not show Hindi or Marathi on 24 November 2026.
- Marathi was not shown in the language list reviewed.
- AARAMORA should link to the official Hindi edition where the source host serves it, and should contact WHO to ask whether Marathi translation/audio is planned or whether an official adaptation partner exists.
- AARAMORA's own Hindi and Marathi scripts require fluent native-speaker, clinician and qualified yoga/mental-health reviewer sign-off before recording or publication.
